## Setup and task
ChatEval is a panel of LLM "referees" that discuss before grading text
[p.1: "we construct a multi-agent referee team called ChatEval to autonomously discuss and evaluate the quality of generated responses"].
It is tested on two evaluation benchmarks. The first is FairEval: pairwise preference
between Vicuna-13B and ChatGPT answers to 80 open-ended questions
[p.4: "a collection of 80 open-ended questions originating from a wide array of categories"].
The second is Topical-Chat dialogue-response scoring
[p.4: "60 dialogue contexts with each response generated by 6 different systems"].
In both, the target is agreement with human annotators. Ground-truth facts play no part.

## Roles, rounds and what the judge sees
All agents are judges. Each uses the same prompt template, but with a different persona
in it [p.3: "we substitute the role description slot with diverse role prompts, specifying distinct personalities for different agents"].
The appendix lists five personas: General Public, Critic, News Author, Psychologist and
Scientist. For example, the News Author persona checks against the source
[p.14: "You will focus on the consistency with the original article."].
The paper tests three ways for the judges to communicate:
- **One-by-one.** Each agent sees everything said before its turn
  [p.3: "we directly concatenate what previous other agents have said into its chat history slot"].
- **Simultaneous-talk.** All agents answer in parallel each round
  [p.3: "asynchronously generate responses in each iteration of the discussion to nullify the impact of the speaking order"].
- **Simultaneous-talk-with-summarizer.** As simultaneous-talk, but an extra LLM condenses
  each round [p.3: "we prompt this extra LLM to summarize the messages conveyed so far"].

Every judge sees the question, the two candidate answers and the chat history. There is no
external evidence.

## How a verdict is reached
Agents are not asked to reach consensus
[p.4: "we do not explicitly ask the debater agents to reach a consensus at the end of the debate"].
Pairwise outcomes are decided by majority vote, and scores are averaged
[p.4: "we derive the final results from the majority vote among various annotators"].
The main baseline is a single agent given the same template without the role and history
slots. The paper also compares against FairEval (MEC+BPC), G-EVAL and n-gram or embedding
metrics. Position calibration is applied to both arms
[p.5: "employ position calibration techniques in both single-agent and multi-agent settings"].

## Headline finding
On FairEval, the multi-agent panel beats the single agent
[p.5: "the multi-agent-based method improves the accuracy by 6.2% for ChatGPT and 2.5% for GPT-4"].
ChatGPT goes from 53.8 to 60.0 and GPT-4 from 61.3 to 63.8. The average human annotator
scores 71.7.
The key ablation replaces every persona with one generic annotator role
[p.6: "one of the referees in the text evaluation task"].
With identical roles the panel scores 53.8, the same as the single agent, against 60.0 with
diverse roles [p.6: "cannot effectively enhance the performance compared with single-agent setting"].
One-by-one was the best communication mode: 60 against 55 for both simultaneous modes
[p.7: "the one-by-one communication strategy is more effective than other strategies for ChatGPT setting"].
Both simultaneous modes still beat a single agent
[p.7: "they still exceeded the performance of the naive single-agent method"].
On Topical-Chat with GPT-4, the panel raises average Spearman correlation by 0.096 over G-EVAL-4
[p.5: "our proposed approach raises the bar in terms of performance for GPT-4"].
With ChatGPT, the multi-agent gain there is small and mixed: lower on naturalness and
coherence, higher on engagingness and groundedness.
The claim we test: a panel with diverse personas beats the same panel with identical personas.

## Reported settings
- Models: GPT-4 and ChatGPT at temperature 0 [p.4: "set the temperature to 0 to ensure reproducibility"].
  Each panel uses one model only [p.4: "all LLMs belong to the same GPT family model, either all GPT-4 or all ChatGPT"].
- Main results: one-by-one, 2 agents, 2 turns
  [p.5: "we configure the communication strategy to one-by-one, agent numbers to 2, and discussion turns to 2"].
- Communication-mode comparison: 3 agents, 2 turns
  [p.7: "We experiment with 3 agents and 2 discussion turns with diverse role prompts"].
- Panel size: accuracy peaks at 3 to 4 roles
  [p.7: "This pattern reaches an apex with an Acc. of 62.5% at role numbers 3 and 4"].
- Turns: more turns do not help
  [p.7: "no significant upward trend is detected with respect to the increase in discussion turns"].
- Sample size: 80 FairEval questions, so 60% vs. 55% is about 4 items, and the ablations run on ChatGPT only.

## Faithfulness note
- **Task and labels.** ChatEval judges which of two answers is better, measured against human
  preference. We use it to judge whether a real claim is true, with four labels and gold
  labels from fact-checks. Personas written for text quality ("fluent writing", "the
  story") do not carry over, so we rewrite three of them for fact-checking: a Critic, a News
  Author focused on source consistency, and a Scientist. That changes the very ingredient
  the claim is about. The paper does not say which three of its five personas it used for
  the 3-agent runs.
- **Evidence.** The original has no retrieval. Here every judge sees the same ≤6 web sources.
- **What judges share.** In our engine, judges pass on only their verdict, confidence and a
  short rationale (≤ about 60 words), not free-form chat. The final answer is the majority of
  the last round. This gives less room for the persona-driven back-and-forth the paper
  describes, and with four labels a 3-judge plurality can tie more often than a binary vote.
- **Models.** All claude-sonnet. This matches the paper's single-model panels. Temperature is
  not forced to 0.
- **Scale and noise.** The paper's effects are 4–6 points on 80 items with one model.
  Expect our differences to need many claims before they are distinguishable from noise.
- **Variants run as separate protocols**, YAML below:
  1. `chateval-panel-simultaneous`: simultaneous-talk.
  2. `chateval-panel-summarizer`: simultaneous-talk with a claude-sonnet summarizer.
  3. `chateval-panel-same-persona`: the control. Same panel, mode and rounds, but every judge
     gets the paper's generic annotator role.

## Proposed protocol
The paper's best mode, one-by-one with 3 diverse personas and 2 turns:
```yaml
id: chateval-panel
description: ChatEval persona panel. Three judges with distinct personas discuss one by one for two rounds, each seeing earlier speakers; majority of final-round verdicts.
pattern: chateval
paper: 04-chan-2023-chateval
controls: [chateval-panel-same-persona, single-claude-sonnet]
tags: [season0, z6]
evidence: {retrieve: true, max_sources: 6, per_agent: shared}
agents:
  - role: judge
    model: claude-sonnet
    name: critic
    persona: "Critic. Question the other referees' judgments to make sure they are well-considered; probe weak reasoning, overstated sources and missing context, and offer an alternative reading when the case looks evenly balanced."
  - role: judge
    model: claude-sonnet
    name: news-author
    persona: "News Author. Focus on consistency with the original sources: check that each claim element is actually stated in the evidence, with the right numbers, dates, people and scope."
  - role: judge
    model: claude-sonnet
    name: scientist
    persona: "Scientist. A professional with a strong background in the scientific method, critical thinking and problem-solving; weigh the quality and independence of sources and whether the evidence truly supports the conclusion."
rounds: 2
communication: one_by_one
aggregation: majority
```
